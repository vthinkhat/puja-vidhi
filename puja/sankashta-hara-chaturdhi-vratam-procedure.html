Sankashta Hara Chaturthi Vratham

Sri Vishnu Roopaya Namah Shivaya

Meaning and Significance

  • Sankashta Hara Chaturthi is observed on the fourth lunar day of Krishna Paksha, after Pournami, every month.
  • Sankashta means Samyak (very great) and Kashtam (trouble).
  • The Vratham is performed seeking Ganapati's grace to remove major troubles and obstacles.
  • According to the Mudgala Purana, the 32nd and final form of Ganapati is Sankashta Hara Ganapati.
    • His right hand is in a blessing posture.
    • His left hand holds a vessel containing kheer.
    • His consort is seated on his left lap.
  • Sankashta Hara Ganapati is especially associated with Krishna Paksha Chaturthi.
  • The Vratham may be started on Krishna Paksha Chaturthi of any month.
  • When Chaturthi coincides with Tuesday, it is called Angaraka Chaturthi and is considered especially powerful.
  • The correct name is Sankashta Hara Chaturthi, not merely “Sankashta Chaturthi”.
  • Anyone may perform the Vratham, regardless of caste, religion, age or marital status.
  • Performing it even once is considered beneficial. Performing it every month, 12 times a year, is said to bring complete success.

13 Benefits and Problems Addressed

  1. Repeated obstacles in work or other endeavours.
  2. Lack of growth in business, career or life.
  3. Nara Drishti or evil eye affecting one's progress.
  4. Financial problems.
  5. Difficulty having children.
  6. Difficulty acquiring one's own house.
  7. Chronic illnesses.
  8. Children struggling in education.
  9. Attacks or problems caused by enemies.
  10. Agricultural losses involving crops or dairy. Farmers may regularly perform the Vratham with a Ganapati idol placed on their farmland.
  11. Delayed marriage.
  12. Troubled married life between a couple.
  13. Unjust punishment for mistakes one did not commit.

Rules and Preparations

1. Choose the Correct Day

  • Perform the Vratham on Bahula or Krishna Paksha Chaturthi.
  • Chaturthi Tithi should be present in the evening around Chandrodaya, the time of moonrise.

2. Morning Bath

Wake early, place black sesame seeds in the bathing water and take a bath.

3. Prepare and Offer the Mudupu

The Mudupu is prepared and offered in the morning. The main Vratham is performed in the evening.

Place the following items inside a red blouse piece or red cloth:

  • Kumkum
  • Turmeric
  • Three double handfuls of rice
  • Two dry dates
  • Two betel nuts
  • Dakshina
  • Tambulam

Tie the cloth into a bundle and place it beside Ganapati. An idol, photograph or printout may be used.

Perform 21 clockwise Pradakshinas around Ganapati. State the purpose of the Vratham and pray for its smooth completion and fulfilment of the intended wish.

4. Fasting

  • Ideally, consume only fruits or milk until the evening Vratham is completed.
  • Healthy people who can fast are encouraged to do so.
  • Children and people with medical conditions, including blood pressure or diabetes, may eat food.

5. Observe Controlled Speech

Silence does not necessarily mean avoiding all speech. It means controlling unnecessary or harmful speech.

  • Avoid gossip.
  • Avoid speaking badly about others.
  • Avoid abusing or insulting others.
  • Speak only when necessary.
  • Keep the mind focused on Ganapati.

6. Evening Preparation

  1. Take a head bath after sunset.
  2. Light Diyas or Deepams.
  3. Tie Torams on the hands.
  4. Begin the Vratham.

Important: Do not remove the Torams until the Puja is completed the following morning.

7. Food and Naivedyam Preparation

Using the rice placed in the Mudupu, prepare:

  1. Bellam Payasam
  2. Twenty-one Undrallu, Kudumulu or Modakas

8. Feeding Others

Anyone visiting the house that day should be offered a meal and regarded as a form of Ganapati.

If nobody visits:

  • Visit a Ganapati temple the following morning.
  • Give a priest or another person enough money to cover a meal.

For the 21 Modakas:

  • Distribute 10 as Prasadam.
  • The remaining Modakas may be consumed by those performing the Vratham.
  • If nobody visits, go outside and distribute the Prasadam.

9. Conduct Restrictions

Observe the following restrictions on the day before and the day of the Vratham:

  • Observe celibacy.
  • Do not consume alcohol.
  • Do not consume meat.

10. Optional Ganapati Homam

Performing Ganapati Homam the following morning is considered beneficial but optional.

  • Perform it yourself if you know the procedure.
  • Alternatively, arrange for a knowledgeable person to perform it at home.
  • If neither is possible, perform Namaskaram and light a Deepam.

Main Vratham: 13 Steps

1. Achamanam, Sankalpam and Kalasharadhana

Begin with the standard preliminary Puja procedures:

  • Achamanam
  • Sankalpam
  • Kalasharadhana
  • Other normal introductory Puja steps

If your family tradition does not use a separate Kalasham, treat the Panchapatra as the Kalasham and perform Kalasharadhana accordingly.

2. Vighna Nivarana Ganapati Puja

Perform Puja to Pasupu, or turmeric, Ganapati to remove obstacles before beginning the main Vratham.

This preliminary Puja is separate from the principal Sankashta Hara Ganapati worship.

3. Shodasopachara Puja to Ganapati

Perform Shodasopachara Puja, including the Upacharas through Pushpa and Abharana.

Dhyanam

Recite the special Sankashta Hara Ganapati Dhyana Sloka from Mantra Shastra and meditate on Ganapati.

Panchamrita Abhishekam

Perform Panchamrita Abhishekam to Sankashta Hara Ganapati.

Five Slokas may be recited as substitutes for the Vedic Panchamrita mantras. These Slokas may also be used while performing Abhishekam for other Devatas.

4. Anga Puja

Perform Anga Puja, worshipping the different limbs and aspects of Ganapati.

5. Ekavimshati Pushpa Puja

Ideally, offer 21 varieties of flowers. If 21 varieties are unavailable, offer 21 flowers of any available variety.

6. Ekavimshati Patra Puja

Traditionally, use the 21 sacred leaves or Patri associated with Vinayaka Chavithi.

  • If all 21 varieties are unavailable, use the available Patri 21 times.
  • If no leaves are available, use Garika or Durva grass, which is considered dear to Ganapati.

7. Ganapati Ashtottaram

Recite the Ganapati Ashtottaram.

8. Sankata Nashana Ganesha Stotram

This is described as one of the most important parts of the Vratham. It is attributed to Narada in the Narada Purana and is regarded as a short but powerful Stotram containing Mantra significance.

Recite it:

  • Once
  • Three times
  • Five times
  • Or as many times as possible

9. Gauri Puja

Perform a brief Gauri Puja because Gauri Devi is worshipped alongside Ganapati.

10. Panchopachara Puja

  1. Dhupam
  2. Deepam
  3. Naivedyam
  4. Mantra Pushpam
  5. Namaskaram

Offer the prepared 21 Modakas, Undrallu or Kudumulu as Naivedyam. Distribute 10 as Prasadam. The remainder may be consumed by those performing the Vratham.

11. Durvayugma Puja

Before beginning the Puja, divide Garika or Durva grass into 11 portions. Each portion may contain two or three stalks.

Recite each of the 11 names of Ganapati and offer one portion of Garika for each name.

12. Read the Four Vratha Kathas

Katha 1: Parvati Devi and the Origin of the Vratham

Parvati Devi was having difficulty getting married. Ganapati explained this Vratham to her and advised her to perform it. She performed the Vratham and subsequently married Lord Shiva.

This Katha also contains details about the Vratham procedure.

Katha 2: Brushunni Maharshi

While Brushunni Maharshi was performing penance, Narada informed him that his wife, children and parents were suffering in Kumbhipakam hell.

The Maharshi sacrificed the spiritual merit and power accumulated through his penance for them. They were liberated from hell and went to heaven.

The meaning presented is that Kumbhipakam is associated with hurting living beings, animals or humans, verbally or physically. The story represents purification from such wrongdoing.

Katha 3: Indra and Nara Drishti

Indra was travelling in his aeroplane when Nara Drishti affected him, causing the aeroplane to fall. Despite people's efforts, it would not move.

The story says that the aeroplane began functioning after air which had touched the corpse of a prostitute reached it.

This is described as an Arthavada, a symbolic story that should not be interpreted only literally.

Its meaning is that even a great person may fall because of Nara Drishti, while the merit arising from performing this Vratham can elevate a person again.

Katha 4: Ghrutaveeryudu

King Ghrutaveeryudu suffered because of a sin committed in a previous life. Despite his efforts, he could not escape the resulting trouble.

He eventually obtained relief by performing Sankashta Hara Chaturthi Vratham. The story represents relief from consequences associated with one's previous wrongdoing.

13. Arghya Offering

12 Arghyas to Ganapati

Prepare water containing:

  • A flower
  • Akshatas

There are three Slokas. Offer four Arghyas for each Sloka.

3 Slokas × 4 Arghyas = 12 Arghyas to Ganapati

Arghya to the Moon and Rohini Devi

Go outside and offer Arghya to the Moon together with Rohini Devi.

Use:

  • Water
  • White rice grains, not Akshatas
  • Some cow's milk

There are three Slokas. Offer one Arghya for each Sloka.

Total: 3 Arghyas to the Moon and Rohini Devi

This offering is associated with the traditional relationship between Ganapati and the Moon.

Completion

The Vratham is completed after the Arghya offerings.

Core sequence: Morning bath with black sesame seeds → Mudupu → 21 Pradakshinas → fasting and controlled speech → evening head bath → Deepam and Toram → 13-step Puja → 21 Modakas → four Vratha Kathas → 12 Arghyas to Ganapati → three Arghyas to the Moon and Rohini Devi → next-morning completion → optional Ganapati Homam.

Sri Matre Namaha
